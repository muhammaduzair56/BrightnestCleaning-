# Cancellation and refund policy research notes

## Sources reviewed

1. GOV.UK / Competition and Markets Authority, [Cancelling goods or services](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cancelling-goods-or-services-guide-for-consumers/cancelling-goods-or-services): cancellation charges and retained deposits should be fair, reasonable, and sufficient to cover direct losses; businesses should take reasonable steps to reduce losses; non-refundable deposits should be a small percentage; charges should be a genuine estimate of direct loss.

2. Citizens Advice, [Cancelling a service you’ve arranged](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/changed-your-mind/cancelling-a-service-youve-arranged/): for many services arranged online, by phone, or by mail order, consumers generally have a 14-day cooling-off period; if the consumer asks for service during that period, the business may keep an amount necessary to cover service provided before cancellation. The guidance gives hiring a cleaner as an example.

3. [Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents): includes rules on information requirements, distance contracts, cancellation periods, exercise and effects of cancellation, reimbursement, and service supplied during the cancellation period.

4. CMA, [Unfair contract terms guidance CMA37](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a609329b00f3323bf1a23f3/unfair_contract_terms_guidance.pdf): consumer contract terms and notices must be fair and transparent; potentially unfair termination and breach terms require careful assessment; the guidance is not a substitute for legal advice.

## Drafting implications

The policy should distinguish statutory consumer rights from BrightNest’s discretionary cancellation window. Any late-cancellation charge should be framed as reasonable and connected to direct loss or reserved staff time, not as an automatic penalty. The policy should explain how customers can cancel or reschedule, when refunds are processed, what happens when BrightNest cancels, and that statutory rights are not limited.

The business-specific assumptions still requiring BrightNest confirmation are the notice window, any fixed or percentage charge, payment/deposit practice, refund processing time, no-show treatment, and contact details. These must be presented as proposed policy terms until confirmed.
